# Little Big Studio

## Updated Brand Brief

## 1. Summary Paragraph

**Little Big Studio is a playful creative studio for visual and digital projects that need character, clarity, and a little more joy. It works across identity, illustration, game art, app concepts, UX reviews, packaging, editorial design, digital visuals, spatial ideas, and creative direction, turning small observations and playful thoughts into visual work with warmth, personality, and care. The brand should feel small by design but big in imagination: personal, flexible, thoughtful, and hands-on, without pretending to be a large agency or overly polished commercial studio. The tone is friendly, clear, and human, while the visual identity mixes bold typography, joyful colour, simple hand-drawn characters, and clean editorial structure.**

---

# 2. Brand Essence

**Little Big Studio** is a playful creative studio with a big visual heart.

It creates visual and digital work for projects, ideas, games, apps, products, stories, spaces, and people that need more personality, more clarity, and a little more joy.

The studio is small by design. It should feel personal, flexible, hands-on, and full of care. The work should feel bigger than the size of the studio: bold in imagination, thoughtful in execution, and memorable in how it looks and feels.

At its core, Little Big Studio turns small observations, odd thoughts, everyday feelings, and playful ideas into visual work with warmth, wit, and character.

## Core idea

**Small by design.
Big where it counts.**

Little Big Studio should feel playful, but not childish.
Personal, but not amateur.
Creative, but not chaotic.
Warm, but still sharp.
Flexible, but not vague.

It is a visual world for ideas that deserve to feel more alive.

---

# 3. Positioning

Little Big Studio is a playful creative practice for projects that need a clear visual voice, strong personality, and thoughtful design.

It works across visual identity, illustration, game art, app concepts, UX reviews, packaging, editorial design, digital visuals, spatial ideas, and creative direction.

The studio should not be positioned as a large agency or traditional full-service design firm. It should feel more like a growing creative practice with range, taste, curiosity, and a strong point of view.

## Positioning statement

**Little Big Studio creates visual and digital work for projects that want to feel human, bold, and a little unexpected.**

It helps ideas, games, apps, products, spaces, and stories find a clearer visual voice.

## Simple one-liner

**Little Big Studio helps ideas, games, apps, products, and stories look and feel more like themselves.**

The voice should be warm, confident, simple, and human.

It should sound creative, but not try too hard.
It should be friendly, but not vague.

Instead of:

### Keep it human

# 9. Creative Range

Little Big Studio works across visual design, digital concepts, and creative direction.

The offer should feel broad and flexible, but not inflated. It is less about being a full-service agency and more about being a playful creative studio with range, taste, and curiosity.

## Visual Identity

Logos, colours, typography, graphic systems, and visual worlds that help a project feel clear, recognizable, and full of personality.

## Illustration

Characters, icons, patterns, scenes, and little visual worlds that add warmth, humour, and human energy.

## Game Art

Playful visual assets, character ideas, interface elements, game screens, props, icons, environments, and concept visuals for games and interactive experiences.

This can include:

* character concepts
* game UI elements
* item icons
* props
* backgrounds
* moodboards
* styleframes
* playful visual systems
* small game-world explorations

## App Concepts

Early-stage visual ideas for apps, digital tools, and interactive products.

This can include:

* concept screens
* app moodboards
* interface explorations
* onboarding ideas
* feature concepts
* visual routes
* playful interaction ideas
* product look and feel

## UX Reviews

A clear creative look at digital products, screens, and flows.

Focused on what feels confusing, flat, inconsistent, or hard to use, with practical suggestions to make the experience clearer and more enjoyable.

This should feel practical, not over-technical.

It can include:

* visual clarity review
* flow review
* hierarchy feedback
* usability notes
* tone and interface consistency
* layout improvements
* small design recommendations
* “what feels off and how to fix it” feedback

## Creative Direction

A visual point of view for projects that need direction.

This can include style, mood, layout, colour, imagery, storytelling, references, and the overall feeling of the work.

Useful for:

* campaigns
* games
* apps
* events
* visual identities
* spaces
* shoots
* presentations
* early-stage ideas

## Packaging Design

Packaging concepts and visuals that feel good in the hand, stand out in the world, and make people look twice.

## Editorial Design

Layouts for publications, zines, guides, lookbooks, presentations, posters, and printed pieces that need rhythm, clarity, and style.

## Digital Visuals

Web, app, social, pitch, campaign, and product visuals that feel clear, warm, and easy to understand.

## Spatial Ideas

Visual ideas for spaces, pop-ups, displays, corners, walls, events, and environments that need atmosphere and character.

## Creative Concepts

Early-stage ideas, experiments, visual routes, and playful concepts that help a project find its shape.

The identity should mix hand-drawn playfulness with clean, confident design.

Think:

**A tidy sketchbook.
A bold poster.
A little character with a big opinion.**

## Overall look

* Bold
* Graphic
* Warm
* Illustration-led
* Slightly imperfect
* Clean underneath
* Clear and confident
* Full of small visual surprises
* Friendly to both print and digital work
* Playful enough for games and concepts
* Structured enough for UX and creative direction

The design should feel playful, but disciplined. Fun elements should sit inside strong layouts, not float around randomly.

# 11. Logo Direction

The logo should make the “little big” contrast visible.

## Logo concept

A stacked wordmark:

**LITTLE**
**BIG**
**STUDIO**

“Little” is smaller.
“Big” is oversized and bold.
“Studio” grounds the composition.

The wordmark should feel handmade, chunky, friendly, and clear. The “BIG” lettering can carry the strongest personality, using a hand-painted marker style with slightly imperfect edges.

## Logo personality

The logo should feel:

* Bold
* Friendly
* Slightly wonky
* Confident
* Easy to read
* Strong in black and white
* Strong in cobalt blue, black, and hot pink
* Good for a website, portfolio, sticker, social icon, poster, app mockup, game screen, label, or notebook

# 12. Mascot Direction

The mascot is a simple rounded blob character.

It should feel like a small visual companion, not a cartoon mascot that takes over the whole brand.

## Mascot traits

* Organic blob shape
* Hot pink hero colour
* Simple black oval eyes
* Curved black smile
* Slightly imperfect outline
* Small black scribble shadow
* Tiny accent marks or sparkles nearby

The mascot should feel friendly, optimistic, and a bit odd. It can appear in corners, stickers, icons, social posts, animations, page transitions, project notes, app screens, game concepts, and visual experiments.

It should represent the brand’s personality:

**small shape, big charm.**

# 14. Colour Palette

The palette should be tight, punchy, and joyful.

## Recommended palette

### Cobalt Blue

Confident, creative, strong.
Used for big type, emphasis, links, graphic moments, and digital accents.

### Hot Pink

Expressive, playful, warm.
Used for the mascot, stickers, emotional highlights, and playful visual moments.

### Warm Yellow

Friendly, sunny, optimistic.
Used as a secondary pop of joy.

### Ink Black

For type, linework, contrast, structure, and facial details.

### Paper White / Soft Cream

For warmth, space, and editorial calm.

## Colour behaviour

Use flat colour.
No gradients.
Strong contrast.
Big blocks when needed.
Plenty of breathing room.

The palette should feel joyful without becoming childish.
Bright, but controlled.
Playful, but designed.

# 15. Typography

Typography should carry the “little big” idea through scale, rhythm, and hierarchy.

## Display type

The main display style should be bold, hand-painted, chunky, and slightly imperfect, inspired by a marker or brush-lettering style.

It should work especially well for:

* BIG
* Headlines
* Short phrases
* Posters
* Stickers
* Hero sections
* Social graphics
* Game title cards
* App concept covers
* Case study headings

## Body type

The body type should be neutral and highly readable.

It should keep the brand grounded while the display type and illustrations bring personality.

## Type behaviour

Use:

* Big headlines
* Short lines
* Stacked phrases
* Strong hierarchy
* Occasional uppercase
* Generous spacing
* Clear body copy

Example:

**SMALL
BY DESIGN.
BIG WHERE
IT COUNTS.**

Then calm body copy underneath.

# 16. Layout & Composition

The layouts should feel editorial, spacious, and well controlled.

This matters because the illustration and typography already bring a lot of energy. The layout needs to bring order.

## Layout principles

* Big type
* Clear sections
* Strong grids
* Simple modules
* Generous white space
* Bold colour blocks
* Illustrations as anchors
* Repeated structures for rhythm
* Strong contrast between tiny and oversized elements
* Clear presentation of process and case studies
* Enough structure for UX reviews and app concepts
* Enough play for game art and illustration

## Useful layout patterns

* Huge headline with tiny icon
* Small caption next to oversized illustration
* Big colour block with simple text
* Three-column creative range list
* Project cards with strong imagery
* “Little / Big” comparison sections
* Sticker-like icon family
* Editorial portfolio pages
* Before / after UX review panels
* App concept screen grids
* Game asset boards
* Moodboard-style creative direction pages

**little movements, big personality.**
