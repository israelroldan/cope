# Due Diligence — Site Visit & Management Interview Notes
## GreenField Logistics B.V. | February 19, 2026
### Attendees: R. Verhoeven, L. Janssen (NorthStar), A. Van den Berg (GreenField CEO), P. Hendriks (GreenField CFO)

---

Started around 9:15, coffee first. Van den Berg gave us a tour of the warehouse in Breda — impressive operation, about 12,000 sqm. He mentioned they added 3,000 sqm in Q2 2024, lease runs through 2029 with an option for 5 more years. Current rent is EUR 14.50/sqm/month which seems below market (Hendriks confirmed — landlord is Van den Berg's brother-in-law, they have a "good arrangement"). Should flag this as related party transaction.

Warehouse employs about 35 people, mostly on permanent contracts. Van den Berg says turnover is low — "maybe 2 or 3 people per year leave" — but when pushed he admitted they lost their head of operations (Jan-Willem Smit) in November and haven't replaced him yet. CFO is currently covering some of those duties. Need to understand the org chart better.

Re: fleet — they own 18 trucks outright (depreciated, avg age 6 years) and lease 12 through LeasePlan. Van den Berg wants to go electric but says "the economics aren't there yet." Current diesel costs are about EUR 680K/year, up from EUR 520K in 2023. He estimates electric would cost about EUR 1.2M upfront per truck but save EUR 15K/year in fuel per truck. No formal business case prepared.

**IMPORTANT** — Hendriks pulled me aside during lunch. He's nervous about the TransNord dispute. Apparently the claim is larger than what's in the data room — he says the real exposure could be EUR 450-500K, not the EUR 320K in the provision. Something about additional penalties for late deliveries that haven't been invoiced yet. He asked me not to raise this with Van den Berg directly. Need legal to look at this carefully.

Financials discussion (afternoon):

Top customers — Hendriks shared a breakdown:
- Albert Heijn (Ahold): EUR 3.2M revenue (2025), contract renews June 2027, 3-year term
- Jumbo: EUR 1.8M, renewed in Jan 2026 for 2 years, BUT they reduced volume by ~15% vs 2024
- Bol.com: EUR 1.4M, new client won in Q3 2024, growing fast, no long-term contract yet (month-to-month)
- HEMA: EUR 0.9M, contract through Dec 2026, some complaints about delivery times
- Action: EUR 0.7M, contract through March 2027
- Remaining ~EUR 2.0M spread across 40+ smaller customers

That puts top 5 at ~80% of revenue. More concentrated than the IM suggested (it said top 10 = 65%).

Van den Berg mentioned they're "in advanced talks" with a major German retailer — wouldn't say who — and expects to sign a EUR 2M+ contract by Q3 2026. He's using this as justification for the earn-out targets being achievable. We should be skeptical until we see something concrete.

IT systems: they run SAP Business One for ERP, custom TMS (transport management system) built by a local IT company in Tilburg. The TMS is critical — handles all route planning, driver scheduling, customer portal. Van den Berg says the source code belongs to GreenField but couldn't produce the IP assignment agreement. The IT company (DigiRoute B.V.) apparently went bankrupt in 2025 and the developer who built it now works as a freelancer. This is a risk — who maintains it going forward?

Hendriks showed us the monthly management accounts. A few things I noticed:
- Revenue recognition looks aggressive on the Bol.com contract — they're booking projected volume rather than actual in some months, then truing up quarterly
- There's a EUR 180K intercompany receivable from "GreenField Vastgoed B.V." — Van den Berg's personal property company. Been outstanding since 2023. Should be treated as a shareholder loan / dividend.
- Insurance costs jumped from EUR 95K to EUR 185K in 2025. Hendriks says this is because of two claims — a truck accident (EUR 340K damage, EUR 200K covered by insurance) and a warehouse water damage (EUR 120K, fully claimed). Premium increase may be structural.
- They have 8 company cars — all BMW 3-series and 5-series. Seems excessive for a logistics company with 52 FTE. Annual lease cost about EUR 112K. Compare this to market rate for appropriate vehicles (probably EUR 60-70K).

Pension: they use an industry pension fund (Pensioenfonds Vervoer). Hendriks confirmed no additional pension obligations or top-up arrangements. Good.

Van den Berg's personal expenses through the company — Hendriks mentioned (again, in confidence) that Van den Berg charges about EUR 35-40K per year in travel and entertainment that is "more personal than business." This has been going on for at least 3 years. Should be normalized.
