Part 1

Grej of the Day — the pedagogical foundation

"Grej of the Day" (Grej av Dagen) was created in 2010 by Swedish teacher Micael Hermansson in Umeå. The Swedish word "grej" means "thing" or "stuff." The method has since spread to 100,000+ teachers across 16+ countries, and a 2024 study of 2,025 respondents confirmed measurable positive effects on engagement and learning behavior.

How a Grej session works

A Grej session runs 7–10 minutes across three acts:

Act 1

The Clue

Day before · ~1 minute

Students receive a cryptic clue — a riddle, image fragment, sound, or quote — designed to create an information gap. Example: "A radiant woman" → Marie Curie. This leverages the Zeigarnik Effect: unfinished cognitive loops persist in working memory.

Act 2

The Micro-Lesson

Next day · 5–8 minutes

The teacher reveals the answer through storytelling-driven presentation using only large, colorful images — zero text. Every Grej must contain at least two genuine WOW moments that trigger dopamine release via the brain's novelty detection system.

Act 3

Retelling + Next Clue

Same day · ~1–2 minutes

Students retell what they learned to a peer, parent, or other adult. This leverages retrieval practice — one of the most powerful learning strategies. The next day's clue is introduced, restarting the curiosity loop.

Why it works — the science

🔍

Information Gap

Curiosity as deprivation — the brain craves closure on open questions (Loewenstein, 1994)

🔄

Zeigarnik Effect

Open cognitive loops persist in working memory, producing anticipation and engagement

⚡

Dopaminergic Reward

Novelty and surprise enhance hippocampal encoding via the brain's reward system (Gruber et al., 2014)

🧠

Retrieval Practice

Retelling activates deep recall — the most powerful learning strategy in cognitive science

"After 100+ Grejs, students can connect almost everything they learn in school to a Grej they've had earlier."

— Ela Strandberg, Teacher, Finland

Part 2

Master taxonomy of 32 canonical activity archetypes

After cataloging 150+ activity types across all platforms, they collapse into 32 distinct interaction archetypes based on core mechanic. Organized by Bloom's taxonomy tier.

Tier 1 — Remember (Factual Recall)
Archetype 1

Multiple Choice (Single Answer)

Kahoot · Quizizz · Duolingo · SoloLearn · Quizlet · Nearpod · Memrise · Blooket
Question with 2–6 answer options; tap one. Immediate feedback. Points for correctness ± speed.
"Which country has the longest coastline?" / "Who painted the Mona Lisa?"
★★★★★ Gold standard
Archetype 2

True / False (Binary Judgment)

Kahoot · Quizizz · SoloLearn · Quizlet
Statement displayed; select True or False. Ultra-fast decision cycle. Ideal for warm-ups.
"The Great Wall of China is visible from space" (False) · "Octopuses have three hearts" (True)
★★★★★ Fastest activity
Archetype 3

Flashcard (Self-Assessed Recall)

Quizlet · Duolingo · Kahoot · Memrise
Card shows prompt on front. User attempts recall, flips to check. Self-sorts into "Know" / "Still Learning."
"What year did the Titanic sink?" [flip] "1912"
★★★★★ Swipe-based
Archetype 4

Image Recognition

Duolingo · Nearpod
See 3–4 images, select one matching a text prompt. Visual association builds vocabulary.
"Which of these is the Colosseum?" (4 famous buildings shown)
★★★★☆ Visual
Archetype 5

Audio Recognition

Duolingo · Kahoot · Memrise
Audio plays (sound, music, speech). User selects correct identification from options.
"What instrument is playing?" / "Which language is this?"
★★★☆☆ Audio assets needed
Tier 2 — Understand (Demonstrate Comprehension)
Archetype 6

Matching Pairs (Association)

Duolingo · Quizlet · Quizizz · Nearpod · Memrise
Grid of 6–12 items; connect related pairs. Matched pairs disappear. Often timed.
Countries↔Capitals · Inventors↔Inventions · Flags↔Countries
★★★★★ Associative
Archetype 7

Categorization / Sorting

Quizizz · Nearpod
Drag items into 2–4 labeled category buckets. Tests conceptual grouping.
Sort animals into Mammals / Reptiles / Birds · Inventions by century
★★★★★ Conceptual
Archetype 8

Fill in the Blank (Word Bank)

Duolingo · Quizizz · Nearpod · Kahoot
Sentence with blanks + word bank below. Tap/drag correct words into blanks. Scaffolded recall.
"The _____ is the largest ocean on Earth" [Pacific/Atlantic/Indian]
★★★★★ Scaffolded
Archetype 9

Sentence / Sequence Reordering

Kahoot Puzzle · Quizizz · Duolingo · SoloLearn
3–6 scrambled items. Drag into correct sequence (chronological, procedural, logical).
Order historical events · Arrange steps of the water cycle
★★★★★ Satisfying UX
Archetype 10

Memory / Concentration Game

Nearpod
Grid of face-down cards. Flip two at a time to find matching pairs from memory.
Match flags to country names · Historical figures to achievements
★★★★★ Working memory
Tier 3 — Apply (Use Knowledge in New Situations)
Archetype 11

Free Recall Fill-in-the-Blank

Kahoot · Quizizz · Duolingo · Quizlet · SoloLearn
Blank with no options provided. Type from memory. Auto-validated with spelling variants. Significantly harder.
"The capital of Australia is _____" / "Element with atomic number 79 is _____"
★★★★★ Strongest retention
Archetype 12

Numerical Estimation / Slider

Kahoot Slider · Quizizz
Move slider along a scale or type a number. Partial credit for proximity. Tactile and engaging.
"In what year was the Eiffel Tower built?" (slider: 1800–2000)
★★★★★ Novel interaction
Archetype 13

Spatial Pin / Hotspot

Kahoot · Quizizz · Nearpod
Tap a specific location on an image (map, diagram). Validated against defined correct zone.
"Tap where Egypt is on this map" / "Tap the heart on this diagram"
★★★★☆ Spatial
Archetype 14

Labeling (Drag onto Image)

Quizizz · Nearpod
Drag text labels from a bank onto correct positions on a reference image. Up to 10 labels.
Label parts of a cell · Continents on a map · Organs of the body
★★★★☆ Snap-to-position
Archetype 15

Scenario-Based Question

Duolingo Stories · Quizizz · Nearpod
Short passage or video + comprehension questions embedded within. Core Grej-style format.
Water cycle paragraph → "Why does water evaporate faster at higher temperatures?"
★★★★★ Grej-native
Tier 4 — Analyze (Examine Relationships)
Archetype 16

Compare and Contrast

Quizizz · Nearpod
Examine two+ items and identify similarities and differences. Structured or free-form.
"How are the Amazon and Nile rivers similar and different?"
★★★★☆ Higher-order
Archetype 17

Find the Error / Odd One Out

Custom implementations
Identify what's wrong or which item doesn't belong. Detective/puzzle feel — highly engaging for kids.
"One of these facts about sharks is false — which one?"
★★★★★ Detective feel
Archetype 18

Cause & Effect / Connection Mapping

Quizizz · Nearpod
Match causes to effects, or find hidden connections between seemingly unrelated facts.
"Match the invention to the problem it solved"
★★★★☆ Relational
Archetype 19

Hot Text (Textual Analysis)

Quizizz
Tap/highlight specific words or sentences in a passage that answer the question.
"Highlight the sentence that explains why volcanoes erupt"
★★★★☆ Reading comp
Tier 5 — Evaluate (Make Judgments)
Archetype 20

Ranking / Prioritization

Kahoot Slider · Quizizz · Nearpod
Rank items by a criterion (biggest→smallest, oldest→newest). Drag-to-reorder.
"Rank these planets from closest to farthest from the Sun"
★★★★★ Judgment
Archetype 21

Poll / Opinion Vote

Kahoot · Quizizz · Nearpod
No correct answer. Select from options or type response. Results displayed as charts or word clouds.
"Which invention changed the world most?" / "What surprised you today?"
★★★★★ Zero grading
Archetype 22

Confidence Rating (Metacognitive)

Kahoot Confidence Mode · The Lost Pyramid
After answering, rate confidence (low/med/high). Higher confidence + correct = more points. Wrong + high confidence = learning moment.
"How sure are you that the Amazon flows through Brazil?"
★★★★★ Metacognitive overlay
Tier 6 — Create (Produce New Work)
Archetype 23

Open-Ended Text Response

Kahoot · Quizizz · Nearpod
Free-form text up to 1,000 chars. AI-evaluated or manually graded. "Show your work" option.
"In your own words, explain why the dinosaurs went extinct"
★★★★☆ Free expression
Archetype 24

Drawing / Sketching

Quizizz Draw · Nearpod Draw It
Digital canvas with pen, highlighter, colors. Draw, label, or annotate on a background image.
"Draw the water cycle" / "Sketch what a Viking longship looked like"
★★★☆☆ Creative
Archetype 25

Brainstorm / Ideation

Kahoot Brainstorm · Nearpod Collaborate
Submit multiple ideas. AI groups by theme. Participants vote on best ideas.
"What are possible solutions to ocean pollution?"
★★★★☆ Collaborative
Cross-Tier — Gamification Wrappers
Archetype 26

Timed Speed Challenge

Memrise · Quizlet · Duolingo · Quizizz
Any question type with countdown timer. Speed + accuracy = score. Creates urgency.
★★★★★ Timer overlay
Archetype 27

Strategy Game Wrapper

Blooket Tower Defense · Gimkit Tycoon · Blooket Café
Correct answers earn in-game currency/resources. Invest in strategic decisions (towers, upgrades, crops).
★★★★★ Pre-built shells
Archetype 28

Collection / Gacha Reward

Blooket Gold Quest · Blooket Fishing · Gimkit Fishtopia
Correct answers grant randomized reward selection with rarity tiers. "One more try" loop.
★★★★★ Reward loop
Archetype 29

PvP Competition

SoloLearn · Blooket Battle Royale · Gimkit Snowbrawl
Direct player-vs-player interaction. Correct answers generate attacks, defenses, or steals.
★★★★★ Social dynamics
Archetype 30

Cooperative / Team Challenge

Gimkit Floor is Lava · Kahoot Submarine · Quizlet Live
Group works together against environmental challenge. Individual answers contribute to shared progress.
★★★★★ Collective
Archetype 31

Social Deduction

Gimkit Trust No One · Blooket Deceptive Dinos
Among Us-style gameplay. Impostors sabotage. Players investigate quiz anomalies and vote.
★★★★★ Social
Archetype 32

Creative Expression (Pictionary / Audio / Video)

Gimkit Draw That · Quizizz Audio/Video · Nearpod Flipgrid
Draw, record audio, or record video to demonstrate understanding. Peers guess or evaluate.
★★★☆☆ Peer interaction

Bloom's taxonomy mapping for AI lesson generation

Mapping cognitive levels to activity archetypes and AI-generability enables an AI lesson engine to automatically select appropriate activity types based on learning objectives.

Bloom's Level Best Activity Archetypes AI Gen. Recommended Use
Remember Multiple Choice, True/False, Flashcard, Image Recognition, Audio Recognition ★★★★★ Warm-up questions; spaced repetition review
Understand Matching Pairs, Categorization, Fill-in-Blank (word bank), Reordering, Memory Game, Scenario Comprehension ★★★★★ Post-content practice; demonstrate grasp
Apply Free-Recall FIB, Numerical Estimation, Hotspot/Pin, Labeling, Scenario-Based Questions ★★★★☆ Core lesson activities; transfer to new contexts
Analyze Compare/Contrast, Find the Error, Cause-Effect Mapping, Hot Text, Ranking ★★★★☆ Deeper engagement; advanced learners
Evaluate Confidence Rating, Poll/Opinion, Ranking by Judgment, Peer Voting ★★★★★ Metacognitive reflection; end-of-lesson
Create Open-Ended, Drawing, Brainstorm, Audio/Video Response ★★★☆☆ Extension activities; student-created Grejs

Key insight: The bottom three levels of Bloom's taxonomy (Remember, Understand, Apply) account for 20 of the 32 archetypes and are nearly fully automatable by AI. These levels also align with the core objective of Grej-style lessons: building broad general knowledge through recall and comprehension of fascinating facts.

Part 4

Research-backed design principles for maximum retention

The testing effect is the single most powerful lever

Retrieval practice produces a full grade-level increase in retention among middle schoolers (Roediger et al., 2011). Children as young as 8 show robust retrieval practice effects independent of reading comprehension or IQ. Fill-in-the-gap tests are particularly effective for primary-school children, with superior performance measured 7 days later.

Every micro-lesson must include retrieval practice, not just content presentation.

The hierarchy of activity effectiveness

  • Spaced retrieval + feedback
  • Free recall with cues
  • MC with corrective feedback
  • Interleaved practice
  • Concept mapping
  • Re-reading ← least effective

Part 5

The AI lesson generation specification

A Grej-inspired AI micro-lesson follows this four-step architecture — 3–5 minutes total, ending before boredom, within the attention window for all target ages.

Recommended lesson flow

Step 1

The Hook / Clue

~30 seconds · Activate

An intriguing image, riddle, or surprising fact that creates an information gap. Push notification the day before or timed reveal at lesson start.

Step 2

The Micro-Content

~90 seconds · Demonstrate

3–5 swipeable image-rich slides with concise narration. No more than 2 sentences per slide. At least 2 WOW facts. Images only, minimal text, storytelling voice.

Step 3

Active Practice

~90 seconds · Apply

3–4 activities ascending Bloom's: True/False → Matching → Reorder → Find-the-Error. Progression from recall to analysis.

Step 4

Reflection + Next Hook

~30 seconds · Integrate

"What surprised you most?" Then reveal the next lesson's clue. Closes the current loop, opens the next one.

Top 12 activity types to implement first

These cover all essential Bloom's levels, are fully AI-generable, and have the strongest evidence for effectiveness:

  1. Multiple Choice
    Universal, proven, trivial to generate
  2. True/False
    Fastest activity, ideal for warm-ups
  3. Fill-in-the-Blank (word bank)
    Scaffolded recall
  4. Fill-in-the-Blank (free recall)
    Strongest retention effect
  5. Matching Pairs
    Excellent for associative knowledge
  6. Sequence Reorder
    Tests deeper understanding of processes/timelines
  7. Numerical Slider
    Engaging for quantitative facts, novel interaction
  8. Find the Error / Odd One Out
    Analytical, detective-like, highly engaging for kids
  9. Categorization / Sorting
    Tests conceptual understanding
  10. Hotspot / Pin on Image
    Spatial knowledge (geography, anatomy, diagrams)
  11. Confidence Rating
    Metacognitive layer on any question type
  12. Poll / WOW Reaction
    Reflection and engagement, zero grading needed

Gamification framework

Streaks XP / Points Knowledge Map Collection / Badges Progress Visualization Curiosity Clue Notifications

Avoid punitive leaderboards for children under 12. Avoid excessive extrinsic rewards that undermine intrinsic curiosity — the core Grej mechanism.

Part 6

Platform-specific innovations worth adapting

Several platforms offer unique mechanics that deserve special attention for a general-knowledge kids' app:

Kahoot

Brainstorm with AI Grouping

Combines idea submission, AI-powered grouping, and peer voting into a single activity. Could power collaborative Grej extensions where students collectively brainstorm connections between topics.

Quizizz

Interactive Video

Pauses video at strategic moments for embedded questions. Maps directly to the Grej presentation format — short video content with comprehension checks.

Blooket

Tower Defense Wrapper

Any question set becomes dramatically more engaging in a strategy game. A "Knowledge Kingdom" mode where correct answers build and defend a civilization could combine knowledge with strategic thinking.

Duolingo

Speech-Based Recall

Showing a prompt and requiring the user to say the answer aloud leverages speech-based retrieval. A "Tell me about" mode where kids verbally explain a topic would be powerful.

Gimkit

Economic Loop

Answer questions → earn currency → buy upgrades → earn more per question. Knowledge literally compounds. Perhaps the most addictive mechanic in educational gaming.

Nearpod

VR Field Trips

360° panoramic exploration could transform geography and history Grejs into immersive explorations — "visit" the Great Wall, the Amazon rainforest, or the surface of Mars.

Conclusion

From catalog to system

This catalog identifies 32 canonical activity archetypes derived from 150+ implementations across 10+ platforms, mapped to Bloom's taxonomy and grounded in cognitive science.

The critical insight: the Grej of the Day method and interactive micro-learning are complementary, not competing, approaches. Grej provides the pedagogical philosophy — curiosity-first, story-driven, short-format, WOW-anchored, socially shared knowledge hooks. The activity catalog provides the interactive assessment layer that transforms passive consumption into active retrieval practice.
