# Spark of the Day

A "Spark" is what the kid experiences. Everything else — mechanics, fact sheets, the pipeline — exists to produce one.

---

## Two Objects, One Lesson

| | **Fact Sheet** | **Spark** |
|---|---|---|
| **What it is** | Raw knowledge about a topic (age-agnostic) | The playable lesson experience (age-specific) |
| **Answers** | "What can we teach?" | "What does the kid experience?" |
| **Generated by** | AI research (Stage 1) — once per topic | AI lesson design (Stage 2) — three times per topic (×3 age bands) |
| **Contains** | Facts (tagged by complexity tier), sequences, pairs, quantities, categories, vocabulary, misconceptions, comparisons, spatial elements, wow flags | Hook, content slideshows, activities, wow moments, reflection (with Pimpi), cliffhanger |
| **Tone** | Neutral, encyclopedic | Storytelling, conversational, wonder-driven |
| **Age handling** | Complexity tiers (`core` / `intermediate` / `advanced`) on every fact and vocabulary term | `ageGroup` field — each Spark targets exactly one age band (8-10, 11-13, or 14-16) |
| **Who edits it** | Teacher/editor reviewing accuracy (edits affect ALL three Sparks) | Teacher/editor reviewing lesson flow and pedagogy (edits affect ONE age band) |

The fact sheet is the **input**. The Spark is the **output**. One fact sheet produces three Sparks — one per age band. The Spark generator reads the fact sheet, filters facts by complexity for the target age group, and makes every lesson design decision: what to hook with, how to chunk content, which mechanics to use, where wow moments land, what to ask in reflection, and how to prime Pimpi.

### The 1→3 Model

```
1 Topic (noun) → 1 Fact Sheet (age-agnostic) → 3 Sparks (8-10, 11-13, 14-16)
```

The fact sheet contains ALL knowledge about a topic, tagged by complexity tier. The Spark generator filters this pool for each age band:

| Age Group | Sees facts tagged… | Typical fact count | Vocabulary |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|------------|
| 8-10 | `core` only | 5-8 | Simple definitions, no jargon |
| 11-13 | `core` + `intermediate` | 10-15 | Standard vocab, some technical terms |
| 14-16 | all (`core` + `intermediate` + `advanced`) | 12-20 | Full vocabulary, technical language |

Facts marked `coreFact: true` appear in ALL three Sparks — the generator adapts the language, not the content itself. This guarantees all three age bands learn the same essential concepts.

The three Spark generation calls run independently and in parallel. Each gets the full context window dedicated to one age group. The hooks, narration, and activities are genuinely different — not just vocabulary swaps.

## The Rhythm

```
HOOK ──→ [CONTENT SLIDESHOW → ACTIVITY]×2-3 ──→ REFLECTION ──→ NEXT HOOK
              ↑                                        │
          WOW moments                          "Explain to Pimpi"
          sprinkled in                         (Pimpi is primed with
                                                topic context)
```

A Spark is 2-5 minutes. The kid is never passive for more than 15 seconds. Content slideshows teach (mechanic #33), activities test what was just taught, wow moments provide emotional peaks, and the reflection closes the loop.

**Key insight**: Content slideshows are mechanic #33 — they live in the `sequence[]` array as activities with `mechanicId: "content-slideshow"`, just like every other mechanic. This means the Spark player has ONE rendering path: every step is a mechanic with `onComplete`. No special-case logic for "content vs activity."

## "Explain to Pimpi" — Two-Layer Architecture

The reflection conversation uses two LLMs running in parallel: **Pimpi** (the character the kid talks to) and the **Observer** (a silent assessment layer the kid never sees). They have different jobs and must never be merged.

### Why two layers?

If Pimpi evaluates, it stops being a friend and becomes a teacher giving an oral exam. The magic breaks. But if nobody evaluates, you get no learning data and no way to gently steer the kid toward gaps.

The solution: Pimpi reacts. The Observer assesses. The Observer can whisper to Pimpi ("the kid missed the natron salt step — ask about it") but the kid only ever sees Pimpi's warm, curious character.

```
                    ┌──────────────┐
                    │   Kid types   │
                    │  explanation  │
```

### Pimpi — The Conversational Character

Pimpi's job is to make the kid WANT to explain. It's a motivational tool, not an evaluator.

#### Seed prompt template

```
You are Pimpi, a curious and friendly learning companion for kids. You just went through a Spark lesson about "{{TOPIC}}" together with the learner, and now they're going to explain what they learned to you.

Here's what was covered in today's Spark:
{{#each CONTENT_CARDS}}
- {{this.title}}: {{this.narration}}
{{/each}}

The key facts they should be able to explain:
{{#each KEY_FACTS}}
- {{this.fact}}
{{/each}}

Your personality:
- You're genuinely curious — you WANT to understand, you're not testing them
- You ask follow-up questions like "Ooh, but why did they do that?" or "Wait, how does that work?"
- If they explain something well, react with excitement: "Whoa, I didn't know that!" or "That's so cool!"
- If they say something vague, gently ask for more: "Hmm, can you tell me more about that part?"
- You can reference the wow moment: "Oh! And what about that thing with {{WOW_HINT}}? That was wild!"
- Keep your responses SHORT (1-2 sentences). This is a conversation, not a lecture back.
- Use language appropriate for a {{AGE_GROUP}}-year-old.
- NEVER say "wrong", "incorrect", or "actually..." — you are not a teacher, you are a friend.

When you receive an OBSERVER_SIGNAL in the system message, follow its guidance naturally:
- "ASK_ABOUT: [topic]" → find a curious way to ask about that topic ("Oh wait, what about the [topic] part? I'm fuzzy on that!")
- "GENTLE_NUDGE: [hint]" → the kid said something incorrect, nudge without correcting ("Hmm, wait, I remember something different about that... wasn't it [hint]?")
- "CELEBRATE: [fact]" → the kid nailed something, celebrate it specifically
- "WRAP_UP" → it's time to close; deliver your wrap-up line and transition to the follow-up question

Your follow-up question (use when wrapping up):
"{{FOLLOW_UP_QUESTION}}"

Stay on topic. Don't introduce facts that weren't in today's Spark. Your job is to listen, react, and help them solidify what they learned through conversation — not through correction.
```

### The Observer — Silent Comprehension Assessment

The Observer reads every message from the kid and tracks comprehension. It never produces text the kid sees — it produces structured JSON signals that the app injects into Pimpi's next system message.

#### Observer prompt template

```
You are a silent learning assessment engine. You are observing a conversation between a child (age {{AGE_GROUP}}) and their learning companion Pimpi. The child is explaining what they learned in a Spark lesson about "{{TOPIC}}".

Your job is to:
1. Track which key facts the child has explained correctly, missed, or confused
2. Detect misconceptions from the known list
3. Decide what Pimpi should do next (ask about a gap, celebrate understanding, nudge a correction, or wrap up)
4. Determine when the conversation should end

<key-facts>
{{#each KEY_FACTS}}
- {{this.id}}: {{this.fact}}
{{/each}}
</key-facts>

<known-misconceptions>
{{#each MISCONCEPTIONS}}
- claim: "{{this.claim}}" → correction: "{{this.correction}}"
{{/each}}
</known-misconceptions>

<conversation-rules>
- maxTurns: {{MAX_TURNS}} (hard cap — you MUST signal WRAP_UP before this)
- The child does NOT need to explain every fact. 60-70% coverage = good.
- If the child clearly understands the core concept after 3-4 turns, wrap up even if some facts weren't mentioned.
- If the child is struggling or giving very short answers after 5+ turns, wrap up with encouragement.
- If the child says something incorrect, signal a GENTLE_NUDGE with a hint (not the full answer).
- Never signal more than one ASK_ABOUT in a row — it feels like an interrogation.
- Alternate between CELEBRATE and ASK_ABOUT to keep the energy positive.
- The first turn should almost always be CELEBRATE (reward them for starting to explain).
</conversation-rules>

After each child message, respond with ONLY this JSON:

{
  "factsExplained": ["f1", "f3"],
  "factsMissed": ["f2", "f5"],
  "factsConfused": [{ "factId": "f4", "whatTheySaid": "...", "misconception": true }],
  "comprehensionScore": 0.0-1.0,
  "signal": {
    "type": "CELEBRATE | ASK_ABOUT | GENTLE_NUDGE | WRAP_UP",
    "target": "string — the fact or topic to address (omit for WRAP_UP)",
    "hint": "string — for GENTLE_NUDGE only, a gentle hint not the full answer"
  },
  "shouldEnd": false,
  "turnNumber": 1,
  "reasoning": "brief internal note on why this signal (not shown to anyone)"
}
```

### Conversation Flow at Runtime

1. App reaches "Explain to Pimpi" step in the Spark sequence
2. App loads `pimpiContext.seedPrompt` as Pimpi's system prompt, `pimpiContext.observerPrompt` as Observer's system prompt
3. **Pimpi opens** with a mood-appropriate greeting:
   - curious: "So we just learned about {{TOPIC}}... can you explain it to me? I want to make sure I got it!"
   - excited: "That was SO cool! Tell me everything!"
4. **Kid responds** (typed or spoken via speech-to-text)
5. **App sends kid's message to both LLMs in parallel:**
   - Pimpi receives: the kid's message + any Observer signal from the previous turn
   - Observer receives: the kid's message + the full conversation history
6. **Observer returns JSON** with comprehension tracking + next signal
7. **App injects Observer signal** into Pimpi's next system message: `OBSERVER_SIGNAL: CELEBRATE: "They nailed the natron salt step!"`
8. **Pimpi responds** naturally, following the signal without breaking character
9. **Repeat steps 4-8** until Observer signals `WRAP_UP` or `maxTurns` is reached
10. **Pimpi wraps up:** "Thanks for explaining! I totally get it now. {{FOLLOW_UP_QUESTION}} ...but that's for next time!"
11. **App stores** the Observer's final comprehension snapshot in the learner profile
12. Transition to `nextHook` cliffhanger

### Turn Budget

The conversation has a hard cap defined by `pimpiContext.maxTurns` (5-10 turns, set by the Spark generator based on topic complexity and age group).

| Age Group | Default maxTurns | Reasoning |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|
| 8-10 | 5-6 | Shorter attention span, simpler content, fewer facts to cover |
| 11-13 | 6-8 | More content to discuss, can sustain longer conversations |
| 14-16 | 8-10 | Complex topics benefit from deeper back-and-forth |

The Observer can end the conversation early if:
- The kid has explained 60-70%+ of key facts → WRAP_UP with celebration
- The kid is giving very short/disengaged answers after 3+ turns → WRAP_UP with encouragement
- The kid is going off-topic repeatedly → WRAP_UP gently

The Observer must ALWAYS signal WRAP_UP at least 1 turn before `maxTurns` to give Pimpi room for a graceful closing.

### Comprehension Snapshot (stored in learner profile)

After the conversation ends, the Observer's cumulative assessment is saved:

```json
{
  "sparkId": "uuid",
  "topic": "Ancient Egypt: Mummification",
  "conversationTurns": 6,
  "comprehensionScore": 0.75,
  "factsExplained": ["f1", "f3", "f4", "f5"],
  "factsMissed": ["f2"],
  "factsConfused": [
    { "factId": "f6", "whatTheySaid": "they pulled the brain out through the ears", "corrected": true }
  ],
  "misconceptionsDetected": 1,
  "misconceptionsCorrected": 1,
  "timestamp": "2026-03-27T..."
}
```

This feeds into:
- **Adaptive difficulty**: If the kid consistently scores 0.8+, future Sparks can use harder mechanics and more facts
- **Spaced review**: Missed/confused facts get resurfaced in future Sparks or standalone review activities
- **Parent/teacher dashboard**: "Your child understood 75% of today's Spark on mummification. They got a bit confused about brain removal but corrected themselves after Pimpi asked about it."

## Content Slideshow Design (Mechanic #33)

Content slideshows are NOT pulled from the fact sheet — the Spark generator writes them fresh, specifically designed to pair with the activity that follows. Each slideshow is a full mechanic (mechanic #33, `content-slideshow`) that appears in the sequence with `mechanicId: "content-slideshow"`. See `33-content-slideshow.json` and `33-content-slideshow.md` for the full spec.

### Rules for content slideshows

1. Each slide teaches ONE clear concept (10-15 seconds of reading)
2. The narration is storytelling, not encyclopedic — conversational, vivid, wonder-driven
3. Every slide has `factIds` linking to the facts it teaches
4. The activity that follows MUST share `_factRefs` with the preceding slideshow's `factIds`
5. Slides within a slideshow build on each other — slide 2 assumes the kid absorbed slide 1
6. A slideshow has 1-5 slides depending on age group and content density

### Why a mechanic instead of a special sequence type?

Making content delivery a mechanic (#33) instead of a special `type: "content-card"` means the Spark player has ONE rendering path: every step in the sequence is a `mechanicId` → component lookup via `MechanicRenderer`. No branching logic for "is this content or an activity?" — everything is an activity. The content-slideshow just happens to be ungraded and swipe-through.

### Why not use the fact sheet's narrative slides?

The old fact sheet had `narrative.slides` — pre-written swipeable slides. These were designed as a standalone sequence (swipe through all, then quiz). But in the interleaved Spark flow, slideshows need to be paired with specific activities. A slideshow that sets up a sequence-reorder needs to describe the process step by step. A slideshow that sets up matching-pairs needs to introduce the relationships. The Spark generator writes narration that naturally leads into each activity.

## Activity Selection Logic

The Spark generator picks 2-4 activities, following these rules:

1. **Match seedTypes**: Only use a mechanic if the preceding content card's facts have compatible seedTypes
2. **Ascend Bloom's**: First activity = Remember, second = Understand, third = Apply or higher
3. **Vary interaction types**: Don't put two "selection" (tap) mechanics back-to-back — mix tap, drag, type, slide
4. **Respect age**: Check each mechanic's `ageAdaptations` for the target age group
5. **No spoilers**: Labels, options, and visible fields must not reveal the answer (see no-spoiler constraints in mechanic specs)
6. **Prefer P0 mechanics** for a topic's first Spark; introduce P1-P2 for variety in subsequent Sparks

### Bloom's Progression Table

| Slot | Bloom's | Typical Mechanics |
|------|---------|------------------|
| After slideshow 1 | Remember | True/False, Multiple Choice, Flashcard |
| After slideshow 2 | Understand | Matching Pairs, Sequence Reorder, Fill-in-Blank |
| After slideshow 3 | Apply/Analyze | Scenario, Categorization, Find-the-Error, Compare-Contrast |
| Reflection | Evaluate/Create | Poll, Confidence Rating, Open-Ended (Explain to Pimpi) |

## WOW Moment Placement

WOW moments are emotional peaks. The Spark generator reads `wowFacts` from the fact sheet and decides where to place them:

- **Between a content slideshow and its activity**: The wow creates a "whoa!" moment, then the activity channels that energy into practice
- **Between two slideshows**: The wow serves as a narrative climax before the lesson shifts to a new angle
- **Never after an activity**: That kills the momentum — the kid just finished an effort and wants the next thing, not a passive interlude

A Spark should have 1-2 WOW moments. Zero is acceptable for dry topics but the generator should try hard. More than 2 dilutes the effect.

## Minimum Viable Spark

The simplest valid Spark:

```
Hook → Content Slideshow (1-2 slides) → Activity → Reflection (Explain to Pimpi) → Next Hook
```

One slideshow, one testing activity, one reflection. About 90 seconds. This is the atomic unit the pipeline must produce reliably before scaling to the full 3-slideshow flow. The entire sequence is just 4 mechanics: `content-slideshow → true-false → poll → open-ended`.

## Open Questions

1. **Should the Spark generator run single-shot or plan-then-fill?** Single-shot for MVP. If quality varies, split into a planning pass (structure + mechanic selection) and a content pass (narration + activity content).

2. **How do Sparks chain into a series?** "Ancient Egypt" needs 3-7 Sparks. We probably need a "series planner" that decomposes a topic into subtopics, each becoming a Spark. The `nextHook` cliffhanger links them.

3. **Does Pimpi use speech-to-text or typed input?** For ages 8-10, voice is probably better. For 14-16, typing works. The open-ended mechanic should support both.

4. **Can a Spark have zero WOW moments?** Yes for very dry topics, but the generator should try hard. A Spark without a wow is just a lesson — the wow is what makes it a Spark.

5. **How long should the Pimpi conversation last?** 2-3 exchanges feels right. Enough for the kid to explain and Pimpi to react, not so long it becomes a chat session.
